##Customer Segmentation — RFM Analysis & K-Means Clustering

## Objective
Segment customers by purchasing behaviour using RFM analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) combined with K-Means clustering, to identify distinct customer groups for targeted marketing. Completed as part of the OASIS internship track.

## Dataset
`Online Retail.xlsx` — UK-based online retailer transaction data (541,909 raw rows).

## Process
1. **Data cleaning**
   - Dropped rows with missing `CustomerID` (transactions can't be attributed without it).
   - Removed duplicate rows.
   - Removed cancelled orders (`InvoiceNo` starting with "C").
   - Removed rows with non-positive `Quantity` or `UnitPrice`.
   - Converted `InvoiceDate` to datetime and derived `TotalAmount = Quantity × UnitPrice`.
2. **RFM feature construction** — for each customer, relative to a reference date one day after the last transaction:
   - **Recency** — days since last purchase
   - **Frequency** — count of unique invoices
   - **Monetary** — total spend
3. **EDA on RFM** — plotted distributions of Recency, Frequency, and Monetary, and checked skewness; all three are right-skewed, with a small group of high-frequency, high-spend customers.
4. **Standardization** — scaled Recency, Frequency, and Monetary with `StandardScaler` so no single variable (e.g. Monetary's larger numeric range) dominates the clustering.
5. **Choosing K** — used the Elbow Method (inertia across K = 2–10) to select **K = 4** clusters.
6. **K-Means clustering** — fit K-Means (K=4, `random_state=42`) on the scaled RFM features and mapped clusters to business-friendly segment names.
7. **Profiling & visualization** — computed mean/median RFM per segment and visualized segment separation with Recency–Monetary, Frequency–Monetary, and Recency–Frequency scatter plots.

## Marketing recommendations by segment
- **VIP customers** — retention-focused: loyalty perks, exclusive offers, early access, personalized recommendations rather than discounting.
- **Loyal customers** — loyalty programs and cross-selling to grow order value and move them toward VIP status.
- **At-risk customers** — reactivation campaigns: personalized offers, reminders, limited-time incentives based on past purchases.
- **Low-value/inactive customers** — low-cost, automated reactivation attempts; keep spend controlled given their low historical value.

## Key insights
- Customers differ substantially in engagement and value — a one-size-fits-all marketing approach isn't well-suited to this base.
- Standardizing RFM features before clustering was essential given their very different scales (days vs. transaction counts vs. currency).
- The Elbow Method gave a data-driven basis for choosing 4 clusters.
- The business's highest-value segment (VIP, 0.3% of customers) contributes disproportionately to revenue — a classic long-tail pattern worth protecting via retention.
- The majority of customers (70.4%) fall into the At-Risk segment, representing the largest opportunity for reactivation campaigns.
